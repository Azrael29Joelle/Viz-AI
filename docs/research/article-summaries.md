## 1. Viz.ai systematic review and meta-analysis

**K. Sarhan *et al.*, "Automated emergent large vessel occlusion detection using Viz.ai software and its impact on stroke workflow metrics and patient outcomes in stroke centers: A systematic review and meta-analysis," *Transl. Stroke Res.*, vol. 16, no. 6, pp. 2258–2271, 2025, doi: 10.1007/s12975-025-01354-0.**
Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12596299/ (corrected; the earlier link pointed to paper 2)

This study investigates the impact of Viz.ai artificial intelligence software on improving hospital workflow and treatment efficiency for patients suffering from large vessel occlusion (LVO) strokes.
The researchers conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of 12 studies involving 15,595 patients. They compared stroke treatment workflow metrics before and after the implementation of Viz.ai.
The findings showed that using the AI platform was associated with significant reductions in the time between CT imaging and the beginning of endovascular treatment. It also improved other important workflow measurements, including door-to-groin puncture time and hospital transfer times.
However, the study did not find statistically significant improvements in final clinical outcomes, such as mortality and functional recovery.
The researchers concluded that integrating AI technology into stroke care can improve hospital coordination and reduce treatment delays, although further research is needed to confirm its impact on long-term patient outcomes.

**Relevance to our project:** this article supports our idea of developing an intelligent hospital coordination system that reduces delays, improves communication between medical teams, and helps patients receive faster treatment.

## 2. AI in a hub-and-spoke stroke system

**"Automated emergent large vessel occlusion detection by artificial intelligence improves stroke workflow in a hub and spoke stroke system of care."** PubMed 34417344. Add the full IEEE details (authors, journal, year, DOI); see issue #5.

This research study examines how artificial intelligence can improve the detection of large vessel occlusion strokes and accelerate communication between medical teams.
The researchers conducted a retrospective study involving 680 stroke cases. They evaluated the use of Viz.ai software to automatically detect emergency stroke cases through medical imaging and notify the relevant medical teams.
The results showed that the median time from CT angiography to medical team notification decreased from 26 minutes to 7 minutes in cases identified by AI. Furthermore, the time from hospital arrival to arterial puncture for transferred patients decreased from 185 minutes to 141 minutes.
These findings demonstrate that AI-powered detection and automated notification systems can significantly improve hospital workflow and reduce delays in emergency stroke treatment.

**Relevance to our project:** this research directly supports our project because it demonstrates the importance of fast medical alerts and effective communication between healthcare professionals. Our proposed system builds on this concept by adding an automatic escalation mechanism that forwards unanswered alerts to another available specialist.
