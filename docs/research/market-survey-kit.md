# Market survey kit (rubric 3.1)

The syllabus asks for a market survey we ran ourselves (questionnaire, observation or interview) and its findings. Nobody has started it yet. Aim for **2-3 short interviews** before Midterm 2.

## Who to talk to
| Person | Why | Done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ER or neurology nurse | Lives the "who do I call now?" moment | [ ] |
| Paramedic or EMS dispatcher | Sees the transfer delay from outside | [ ] |
| Hospital manager or stroke coordinator (if reachable) | The one who would pay | [ ] |
| Someone whose relative had a stroke (optional) | The human side | [ ] |

## Rules
- Do not ask for or write down patient data, real names or the hospital's name unless the person agrees.
- Say that it is a student project and the answers are anonymous.
- Take notes by hand or with the person's consent. No AI tool on the raw notes if they contain personal details.

## 10 questions (about 10 minutes)
1. What is your role, and how many stroke or emergency alerts do you deal with in a month (roughly)?
2. When an urgent alert goes out, who gets it and how (pager, call, app, message)?
3. What happens when the person does not answer? Who notices, and after how long?
4. Can you remember a case where the answer came late? What was the cause?
5. How do you track where the minutes go today (by hand, spreadsheet, system, not at all)?
6. Do you already have a tool that escalates an unanswered alert? Which one?
7. Which is worse for you: too many alerts, or one alert nobody answers? Why?
8. Would an automatic "backup doctor after 5 minutes" rule help, or cause problems? What would the time limit be?
9. Who in the hospital decides to buy a tool like this, and what would they need to see first?
10. What would make you refuse to use it?
